Finance Therapy Session · the intervention framework

Three Arms, One Schedule

How a participant moves from enrolment to the exit survey, what is fixed on the day they are assigned, and how the ten sessions are filled.

1. The run, for one participant

Two participants. One's investment mistake is found at month 3 and she enters an arm then; the other's is found at month 6 and he enters then. Both get the same ten sessions, counted from their own entry day.
Day 0
Enrol
Baseline surveys, stake rate drawn, simulated account.
Months 0–3
Diagnose
Free trading, 12 weekly checkpoints, no programme. Three mistakes measured.
When a mistake is found
Randomise
At least one mistake: one of three arms, stratified. None: keep trading, re-checked, enrolled when one appears.
Next 19 weeks
Ten sessions
Every 14 days, ~15 min, counted from each person's own assignment day. Trading continues.
After session 10
Exit survey
Fixed interval after session 10, or a calendar date.

Sessions are anchored to the assignment day, so the dose is identical whenever a person enters. Only the calendar period differs, which batch-wise randomisation and a batch fixed effect absorb.

2. What is fixed at assignment, and what is not?

Frozen at assignment

  • The arm, by randomisation.
  • The mistake profile: which of the three mistakes, and each one's weight (section 3).
  • The allocation: which mistake each of the ten sessions covers, and in what order (section 3).
  • The schedule: ten sessions, 14 days apart, from this day.

Keeps flowing, but never feeds back into the plan

  • Trading: recorded continuously; it is the outcome.
  • Session completion: offered on schedule, catch-up allowed in order; actual completion is a recorded engagement variable.
  • Recent trades to the AI agent (Arm 3 only): the conversation starts from them; the topic, count and order of sessions do not move.
A participant whose mistake disappears mid-course still completes the course. The later sessions are maintenance. Stopping early would make the dose a function of the outcome and break the across-arm comparison.

3b. The course: one track per mistake, ten cards each

"Card" is a metaphor: one card is the material for one session, about 15 minutes of lesson, worked example, practice with feedback, and a rule and plan. Three tracks of ten cards each, written once. Every card has the same shape and length.

(a) One programme for everyone

  • Ten cards to write.
  • Matches the grant's wording: "covering the same three mistakes".
  • Participant A spends 6 of 10 sessions on mistakes she does not make.
  • Arm 3 is targeted by construction, so Arm 3 − Arm 2 mixes "targeted" with "conversational".

(b) Targeted by profile proposed

  • Thirty cards to write, each once.
  • Every session is on a mistake the participant makes: more dose per hour, more power.
  • Cores first, in severity order, then follow-ups.
  • Arm 3 − Arm 2 differs only in delivery, which is RQ1. Recorded as an amendment to the grant text.

3d. Arm 3 keeps the same slot plan; only the delivery changes

Arm 3 does not get a curriculum of its own. The AI agent works through the same slot plan as Arm 2, card by card, so Arm 3 versus Arm 2 differs only in how a card is delivered.
